
# Study Plan Overview

A strong HFT preparation plan is not just a topic list. It is a sequencing problem: fix the weakest bottlenecks first, then convert knowledge into interview-ready recall.

## Priority Order

1. **Behavioral stories** — these are easy to underestimate and hard to improvise well.
2. **C++ fundamentals under pressure** — ownership, atomics, memory layout, and concurrency.
3. **Systems basics** — CPU caches, scheduling, paging, sockets, and latency sources.
4. **Low-latency design** — deterministic paths, tail latency, and observability.
5. **Trading context** — enough microstructure understanding to reason about engineering choices.

## 7-Day Emergency Mode

- Spend the first two days building a reliable behavioral story bank.
- Use days three to five for C++, systems, and low-latency fundamentals.
- Use the final days for mock interviews, review, and targeted gap closure.

## 30-Day Balanced Mode

- **Week 1:** Behavioral foundations, study plan setup, and core C++ refresh.
- **Week 2:** Systems and networking review with short daily coding drills.
- **Week 3:** Low-latency design, order flow, instrumentation, and performance tradeoffs.
- **Week 4:** Mock interviews, weak-topic review, and answer tightening.

## Daily Cadence

- **30 minutes:** Flash review of yesterday's weak answers.
- **60 minutes:** Deep work on one technical topic.
- **30 minutes:** Behavioral story refinement.
- **20 minutes:** One timed question or coding drill.
- **10 minutes:** Update progress and write what still feels shaky.

## What Good Progress Looks Like

- You can answer fundamental questions in under two minutes without rambling.
- Your behavioral answers contain concrete scope, constraints, and outcomes.
- You can explain not only what a design does, but which latency and correctness risks remain.
