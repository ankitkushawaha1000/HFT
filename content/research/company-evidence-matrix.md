## Interpretation notes for candidates

### 1. Absence of official detail does not mean absence of evaluation
For firms such as Citadel or Jump, sparse official interview detail should not be misread as a lighter process. In practice, sparse official documentation usually means **prepare broadly and rely less on exact-process speculation**.

### 2. Experienced candidates should assume deeper probing
Even when public pages do not explicitly say “system design” or “advanced concurrency,” experienced SWE candidates should assume interviewers may probe architecture, tradeoffs, and production judgment.

### 3. C++ depth varies by role family
At some firms, especially Jane Street and Two Sigma, general software-engineering reasoning may be more publicly emphasized than C++ specifically. At others, especially Citadel Securities, Optiver, and HRT-adjacent low-latency roles, strong C++ depth is more clearly implied.

### 4. Behavioral rounds matter everywhere
Even where official evidence is limited, every company in this matrix hires into collaborative, high-performance environments. Candidates who prepare only technical questions are underprepared.

---

## Practical takeaway

Across all six firms, the safest high-confidence preparation set is:

- clear motivation for the move into HFT or quantitative trading;
- strong coding and C++ fundamentals;
- production-systems judgment under pressure;
- direct, evidence-based behavioral stories;
- system design reasoning for performance-sensitive, correctness-critical systems.

The exact round order may vary. The underlying signal targets are much more stable.
