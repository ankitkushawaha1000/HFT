# Optiver Recruiter Screen Guide

## What the recruiter is really assessing

The recruiter screen is usually the smallest stage in time and the biggest stage in terms of funnel efficiency. Recruiters are not trying to verify your deepest systems knowledge; they are trying to answer a narrower set of questions quickly:

- Is this candidate plausibly aligned to the role and level?
- Can they communicate clearly and concisely?
- Do they understand why Optiver is distinct from a generic software job?
- Are compensation, location, visa, and timeline likely workable?
- Does the candidate sound compatible with a direct, high-performance culture?

A strong recruiter call should leave the recruiter thinking, "This person is credible, motivated, and worth the team's time."

## Ideal length and format

- **Typical length:** 20-30 minutes
- **Format:** phone or video
- **Pacing:** 3-5 minutes on your background, 5-10 minutes on interest and fit, 5-10 minutes on logistics and candidate questions

If you need a target, aim for answers that are crisp and expandable. The recruiter should not need to drag clarity out of you.

## Typical recruiter screen questions

### Background and fit

- "Can you walk me through your background?"
- "What are you doing now, and what kind of problems do you own?"
- "Why are you considering a move?"
- "What type of role are you targeting next?"

### Motivation for Optiver

- "Why Optiver specifically?"
- "What interests you about trading systems or market infrastructure?"
- "What attracted you to this team?"

### Seniority calibration

- "Have you led projects or mentored others?"
- "How much cross-team work is part of your current role?"
- "What's an example of a high-stakes system you worked on?"

### Logistics

- location,
- work authorization,
- interview availability,
- compensation expectations,
- notice period.

## A good structure for your opening pitch

Use a 60-90 second arc:

1. **Present role and scope** - what you own now.
2. **Relevant history** - prior systems, scale, or business context.
3. **What differentiates you** - incidents, reliability, performance, leadership, or cross-team work.
4. **Why now / why Optiver** - one or two specific reasons.

### Example skeleton

```text
I'm currently a senior engineer working on CANDIDATE_TODO high-stakes systems at Deutsche Bank, with a focus on CANDIDATE_TODO. Over the last CANDIDATE_TODO years, I've worked on systems where reliability, latency, and change safety matter a lot, and I've increasingly taken on cross-team ownership around CANDIDATE_TODO. I'm exploring roles where engineering decisions are closer to real-time outcomes, and Optiver stands out because of its emphasis on high-performance systems, direct collaboration, and technical ownership.
```

## How to discuss your Deutsche Bank background

A banking background can be a strength if framed well. The risk is sounding slow-moving, overly process-bound, or too far from hands-on engineering.

### Emphasize these angles

- **Critical systems discipline:** correctness, resilience, controls, auditability, and operational rigor.
- **High-stakes environment:** mistakes can carry real financial, operational, or regulatory consequences.
- **Complex stakeholder management:** navigating product, risk, operations, and engineering.
- **Modernization or scale work:** migration, platform improvements, incident prevention, automation, performance tuning.

### Avoid over-indexing on these angles

- Long descriptions of committee process
- Too much institutional jargon
- Framing yourself as primarily a coordinator rather than an engineer
- Suggesting that speed and directness are culturally uncomfortable for you

### Better narrative

"My Deutsche Bank experience gave me a lot of exposure to complex, high-consequence systems where reliability and risk communication really matter. What I want next is an environment where that rigor combines with faster technical feedback loops and tighter connection between engineering choices and outcomes."

## How to frame your interest in HFT or trading systems

You do not need to claim a lifelong dream of market making. A better approach is to connect your real engineering preferences to the domain.

### Strong motivation themes

- You enjoy systems where performance and correctness both matter.
- You like fast feedback loops.
- You want tighter coupling between engineering quality and business outcomes.
- You are energized by technically strong peers and direct problem solving.
- You are curious about how market structure and system behavior interact.

### Weak motivation themes

- compensation first,
- prestige first,
- vague statements like "finance seems exciting,"
- superficial references to AI or quantitative trading with no connection to your own work.

## Compensation and negotiation timing

The recruiter screen is usually for **range alignment, not hard negotiation**.

### Best practice

- If asked early, give a thoughtful range or say you want to understand level and scope first, while confirming you expect competitive market compensation.
- Save detailed negotiation for after mutual interest and level calibration are clearer.

### Good phrasing

```text
I'm looking for a package that's competitive for a senior engineering role of this scope. I want to make sure I understand level, responsibilities, and location mix before narrowing to a specific final number, but I'm happy to discuss broad expectations.
```

## Red flags that get candidates filtered out

- Cannot explain resume clearly
- Generic "why us" answer that could fit any company
- Sounds interested only in compensation
- Weak or confusing communication
- No evidence of ownership at the stated level
- Defensive tone about incidents, feedback, or changing jobs
- Compensation expectations wildly outside role band with no flexibility

## Questions you can ask the recruiter

Good recruiter-screen questions:

- "How is the interview process structured for this role?"
- "What tends to distinguish candidates who do well?"
- "How is this team thinking about level and scope for the hire?"
- "What should I understand about the team's operating style before I meet interviewers?"

These show maturity and help you prepare.

## Final checklist

Before the call, be ready to answer:

- Why this role?
- Why now?
- Why Optiver?
- Why are you credible for a senior role?
- What from Deutsche Bank transfers directly?
- What kind of environment do you want next?

If you can answer those six questions crisply, your recruiter screen will likely do its job: open the door to the real evaluation rounds.
