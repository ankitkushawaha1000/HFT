# SIG (Susquehanna International Group) — Round 4: Superday

**What this round assesses:** Algorithms, C++ internals, probability/expected value, decision-making under uncertainty, poker/trading-game reasoning, and behavioral fit.

## Format

5–6 onsite rounds over about 5–6 hours. Reported structure includes coding, C++ deep dive, probability/EV, poker or trading-game session, and behavioral/senior leadership discussion.

## Games / probability

SIG's Superday is famous for probability, expected-value, and poker-style decision rounds. Reports describe No-Limit Texas Hold'em or simpler trading/card-game alternatives, with evaluation focused on EV thinking, pot odds, Bayesian updating, bankroll/risk management, composure, and information asymmetry.

## Representative questions

1. "Implement a priority queue. Now explain how `std::make_heap` works internally." `anecdotal`
2. "Find the shortest path in a maze where some cells have time penalties." `anecdotal`
3. "Design an algorithm for calculating the N-th Fibonacci number in O(log N) using matrix exponentiation." `anecdotal`
4. "Explain how `std::function` works internally. What is type erasure and what are the performance costs?" `anecdotal`
5. "Explain perfect forwarding. Write a wrapper function `callWithLogging(f, args...)` that forwards arguments to f and logs before/after the call." `anecdotal`
6. "What is undefined behavior (UB) in C++? Give 3 examples of common UB in production code and how to avoid each." `anecdotal`
7. "A fair die is rolled. You can either take the dollar value shown or pay $1 to roll again (up to 3 rolls total). What is the optimal strategy and expected payout?" `anecdotal`
8. "You flip a fair coin. If heads: you win $3. If tails: you flip again. If heads on second flip: you win $1. If tails on second: you lose $2. What is the EV? Would you play if offered?" `anecdotal`
9. "You are a market maker. I want to buy 10 shares of a stock you don't know the fair value of. What bid/ask spread do you offer and why?" `anecdotal`
10. "What is the expected number of cards you must draw from a shuffled deck before you see the first ace?" `anecdotal`
11. "Do you think probabilistically?" (Are you making pot-odds calculations?) `anecdotal`
12. "Do you manage risk appropriately?" (Not going all-in recklessly, not folding every marginal edge.) `anecdotal`
13. "Can you update beliefs quickly?" (Bayesian thinking as the board changes.) `anecdotal`
14. "Are you emotionally stable?" (Tilt, frustration management.) `anecdotal`
15. "Do you understand position and information asymmetry?" `anecdotal`
16. Playing tightly and correctly is rewarded more than aggressive bluffing. `anecdotal`
17. Some reports suggest SIG uses a simpler "trading card game" or simulated market as an alternative or supplement for technology-track candidates who may not know poker. `anecdotal`
18. "SIG values independent thinking. Tell me about a time you pushed back on a direction from management with data." `anecdotal`
19. "What do you know about how options market making works? How would you explain delta and gamma to a non-finance person?" `anecdotal`
