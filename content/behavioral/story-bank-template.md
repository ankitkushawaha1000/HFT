# Story Bank Template

Use this worksheet to build a reusable bank of stories before interviews. The goal is not to memorize scripts. The goal is to prepare evidence. Each story below should be real, specific, and adaptable across multiple prompts.

## How to fill this out

For every story, capture:

- **Situation:** What was happening? Why did it matter?
- **Task:** What specifically needed to be achieved or resolved?
- **Action:** What did you do? What tradeoffs did you make?
- **Result:** What changed, with numbers if possible?
- **Learning:** What did you change afterward?
- **Quantified impact:** latency, uptime, incident reduction, time saved, revenue/risk proxy, or scope.

Add concrete Deutsche Bank examples wherever possible.

## How to adapt one story to multiple questions

For each story, write three versions:

1. **Ownership lens** - what you drove.
2. **Collaboration lens** - who you aligned with.
3. **Learning lens** - what changed in your behavior later.

Example: the same Deutsche Bank incident can answer questions about ownership, conflict, prioritization, risk communication, and mentoring depending on which part you emphasize.

## Story completeness checklist

- [ ] Clear stakes in the first two sentences
- [ ] Your role distinguished from team role
- [ ] At least one explicit tradeoff
- [ ] Measurable or scoped result
- [ ] Credible learning, not a cliché
- [ ] Adaptable to at least three questions
- [ ] Contains no confidential details you should not disclose

## Final tip

A strong story bank turns behavioral interviews from improvisation into selection. You are not trying to invent answers live; you are choosing the best evidence for the question in front of you.
